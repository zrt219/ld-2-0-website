PROFESSIONAL SCHEDULING & PHYSIOLOGY Back-to-Back. Again. Should Recovery Time Have a Greater Place in Professional Scheduling? Flying from Denver to Minneapolis at two in the morning, arriving at the hotel at four, and stepping onto the court fourteen hours later to sprint four miles against elite athletes is not high performance. It is biological survival. Back-to-back games remain one of the primary drivers of soft-tissue strains, hamstring tears, and cognitive fatigue across professional basketball. Lornette Daye's background in Olympic sprinting and biomechanics emphasizes the scientific reality of the central nervous system. When the nervous system is chronically depleted, reaction times drop by milliseconds, joints lose neuromuscular protection, and catastrophic non-contact injuries skyrocket. More recovery does not weaken the game. More recovery creates a faster, cleaner, more explosive product that protects the athletes fans pay to see. Respect the science of human recovery. THE 82-GAME TOLL 82 Games. One Body. Is the Modern NBA Regular Season Asking Too Much of Its Athletes? The eighty-two-game regular season schedule was established in 1967. Yet the calendar remains unchanged. Lornette Daye advocates for institutional courage in sports governance. The athlete is more important than the broadcast schedule. When leagues prioritize commercial volume over human biological sustainability, the fans end up watching depleted rosters in playoff games. A schedule must serve the athlete, not the other way around.
